The following year the ministerial Committee used the results and set up a program of action aiming at preserving the European cultural inheritance and to draw closer the bonds of culture in order to develop and to make Europe only one cultural entity, without sacrificing its great variety. In June 1955 an important cultural discussion took place which prompted very interesting statements regarding cultural relations between East and West.

The country members realise that without a very strong spiritual community it is practically impossible to arrive to a political community, a block of countries.

The carrying into effect of a program of action caused the European Council to be the centre of the numerous cultural tests from the European standpoint.

The creation on January 1st 1959 of the European Culture Fund is the start of a new and very important stage. The Fund's Managing Committee count both Government representatives and parliamentaries. They cooperate in setting up programs and granting financial means. Moreover, the Managing Committee is the first Consulting Assembly's organisation qualified for carrying out decisions.

The Assembly is of opinion that the Cultural Fund should enable the European Council to work out a large cultural program. The European Council should become the mainspring of European cooperation, both at Governments and at private levels.

An important step in this direction is the creation of Committees for scientific research, the transfer of cultural activities from the Union of Occidental Europe to the European Council and agreement between the European Cultural Fund and the one created by Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands.

The Managing Committee has set up a program strongly backed up by the Consulting Assembly. It includes universities, youth, tuition, representation of Europe towards non Europeans. One of the essential tasks of European cultural cooperation will certainly be to define the part which Europe will have to play in a world in which it has lost its former place and which is facing several cultures.

At a time when Europe is still economically divided and when, due to quite a number of serious political questions, divergent standpoints and interests are a hindrance to a total union, it seems that culture is the only ground for understanding between European nations.

Dr L. Rehling
