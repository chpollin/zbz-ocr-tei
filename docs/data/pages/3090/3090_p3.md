PROF. DR. JEANNE HERSCH, UNIVERSITY OF GENEVA

# Karl Jaspers and his Philosophy

It was by pure chance that I went to Heidelberg at the age of eighteen to learn German. It was my first term away from home. Since my main subject in Geneva had been Latin, I joined a Latin seminar in Heidelberg. We were reading Virgil’s Georgics and I can still remember our endless discussions on the following question: after a number of gods have been evoked by name at the beginning of the first Book, comes the passage “… and ye all, gods and goddesses;” our problem was to decide whether the expression “ye all” included the deities already mentioned, or whether it referred only to the others, i.e. those not yet named. While this discussion was going on, I used to count the clouds sailing across the sky as they passed the window of our classroom.

One day, as I was walking through our beautiful old refectory (formerly the castle stables), I met a girl who asked me whether I had heard Jaspers yet – she herself went to hear him from time to time. I said, “No, who is he?” She answered lightly, “I’d drop in there, if I were you – it’s interesting.”

I went. The lecture was in the old auditorium maximum. There, for the first time, I heard German as it is spoken in the North – with its clear, as it were transparent, s-p, s-t, sprechen, stehen. And there for the first time I saw that uplifted hand, that long hand, two fingers outstretched, two fingers folded back – which has always remained for me as a symbol that whatever is said in philosophy is not yet the last word.

At that time I had no more than the faintest glimmerings of philosophy. My German was very poor. I understood hardly anything of the lecture; but I did realize clearly that there was something here to be understood.
