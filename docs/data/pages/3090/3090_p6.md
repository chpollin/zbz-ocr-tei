To what then is one bound? That is the question put ironically by so many believers. Do we bind ourselves to reject every obligation and to preserve nothing but the tolerance that enables individuals and nations with differing ideals to exist side by side without violence — i. e. the agnosticism of the Enlightenment? That would merely mean preferring peace to truth, and even sacrificing truth to peace. But for Jaspers there can be no true peace that is not constantly nourished on a passion for truth.

Philosophical obligation is an “objectless” obligation, just because it denotes obligation to the truth. Truth — if that is what we are genuinely seeking, not some kind of substitute for it — is something that nobody can ever possess. This is not due to our lack of knowledge. On the contrary: the more we know, the longer grow the paths stretching into infinity which open up to us new, hitherto unknown, vistas. The reason lies in the nature of Truth itself. To us only objects are known; but Truth is not an object. Nor is it a subject. It is the horizon of Being into which every kind of established, acquired, and formulated knowledge is assimilated within that realm of Being that comprises a subject directed to an object. All objective knowledge leads towards and strives to attain this horizon of Truth. Only if we are suffering from a paralysis of our desire for the truth can we be satisfied with specialized or general knowledge whose meaning is restricted to a particular point of view and particular methods; only then could we mistake this for Truth itself.

Philosophical obligation has, therefore, no object, because Truth cannot be an object.

But although it has no object, it nevertheless involves a pledge — and this pledge is absolute. For Truth merges with Being. Philosophical commitment — because it cannot reach its appropriate object (since this “object” cannot be an object) — finds itself so to speak sentenced to a pledge which is being. Not the Being that transcends the division into subject and object — in which the commitment dimly divines its own ultimate origin —, but the Being of the individual committed, who binds himself to exist.
