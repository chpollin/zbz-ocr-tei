But when the subject tries to acquire a knowledge of the world, what it aspires to is not this practical, vital knowledge any more. It wants to get a knowledge independent of its situation and interests, an apodictic knowledge, valid for everyone, justified by rationality and empirical verification. In this kind of research, it does not act as Dasein anymore, but as what Jaspers calls “Bewusstsein überhaupt”, that is to say “consciousness in general”, which is impersonal, common to any human subject as such, in so far as it wants to acquire objective knowledge.

And that is not enough. As a subject, I want to interpret the world with the help of great unities and meanings. I am not alone. I belong to a culture which tends to impose its schemes, demands, values. I don’t have to accept them as they are, I can change and correct them for good reasons, but I cannot do without them. That means that as a subject towards the world, I am not only Dasein, not only consciousness in general, but also, as Jaspers says (using here in a somewhat polemical way an expression of Hegel’s) “Geist”, “spirit”, aiming at global meanings and unities.

The subject fails, it cannot reach its own unity. As you can see, the first gesture of Karl Jaspers towards unity and totality fails, not only on the side of the world, but also on the side of the subject, of the self. But for Jaspers, the experience of failure at a limit which cannot be transgressed is always a creative experience. The limit itself is not like a dead wall, but it has a “beyond” which, through our failure, affects us. On the side of the subject, this “beyond” is what Jaspers calls “Existence”, and on the other side, the side of what the subject is not, is what he calls “Transcendence”, or often also “God”.

For Jaspers, “Existence” means, roughly speaking, authentic freedom, it is the emergence of a free decision, *hic et nunc*, in which the self commits itself absolutely. It is not something which could be grasped or stated like a fact or phenomenon, and therefore Jaspers speaks mostly when he is not “living existentially” but only speaking or writing about Existence, of “possible Existence”. It means that, in theory, Existence is only a possibility. It is real in the actual decision. The failure in the theoretical attempts to reach being as a unity in the world or in the subject is in itself a call for Existence to actualize itself “beyond” any objectivity or subjectivity (in a psychological sense). Jaspers says: “Es appelliert an Existenz”.

Now it would be wrong to think that Existence, awakened by the failure against the limit of empirical reality, is not in the world of space and time any more. On the contrary: an existential decision is always precisely connected with a concrete given situation. It cannot be generalized. It is only valid *hic et nunc*.
